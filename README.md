# Explainable-Deepfake-Video-Detection-Using-Multiple-Cues
The rapid proliferation of deepfake technology—driven by advances in generative adversarial networks, autoencoders, and diffusion-based text-to-video models—has created an unprecedented threat to information integrity across journalism, legal proceedings, social media, and personal privacy. Modern deepfakes range from traditional face-swap and expression-reenactment manipulations to fully AI-generated videos produced by tools such as Sora, Pika, and CogVideo, making it increasingly difficult for both humans and automated systems to distinguish authentic media from fabricated content. Existing detection approaches predominantly rely on a single forensic cue, such as spatial artifacts or frequency-domain anomalies, which renders them inherently brittle against compression artifacts encountered on social media platforms and vulnerable to novel generation techniques that leave no trace in the specific signal being analyzed.

This project presents a Multi-Cue Deep Learning Framework for Deepfake Video Detection that addresses these challenges by combining six complementary forensic detection cues—visual, temporal, remote photoplethysmography (rPPG), frequency, audio-visual synchronization, and perceptual trajectory straightening (ReStraV)—into a unified, attention-based fusion architecture. Each cue independently analyzes a distinct dimension of video authenticity: spatial face artifacts, cross-frame temporal coherence, physiological plausibility, spectral manipulation fingerprints, lip-sync consistency, and AI-generated motion characteristics, respectively. The system is trained and evaluated on four large-scale benchmark datasets—Google DFD, FakeAVCeleb, FaceForensics++ (C23), and VidProM—encompassing over 97,000 videos across seven or more manipulation types and seven AI-video generators.
